Russell secured pole by 0.064 s — the equivalent of a single front‑wing click. Mercedes maximised the W15’s single‑lap envelope at Barcelona, locking out the front row with Antonelli third, yet race pace diverged sharply. Hamilton’s SF‑24 displayed superior tyre degradation control over the 66‑lap distance; the qualifying gap evaporated within the opening stint. By lap 12 Hamilton sat in DRS range, forcing an early Mercedes stop that compromised Russell’s second stint. Hamilton’s 1:18.887 fastest lap on worn hards with eight laps remaining confirmed the SF‑24’s race‑day efficiency window exceeded the W15’s.

Mercedes’ qualifying edge stemmed from a low‑drag rear wing tuned for clean air. In traffic that aero balance cost Russell traction exiting Turn 10 and Turn 14, where Hamilton consistently gained ~0.2 s per sector. Ferrari’s higher‑downforce spec preserved rear stability through the high‑speed sequence, enabling later braking into Turn 1 — the primary overtaking zone.

Antonelli converted P3 into a solid data day; his race pace matched Russell until a slow pit stop dropped him out of the points. The evidence is clear: Mercedes wins Saturday, Ferrari wins Sunday. Development focus for Silverstone must shift toward race‑trim aero balance and tyre management.
